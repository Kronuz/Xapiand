## Rulings I made

1. Rules come from ICU's compiled word.brk state machine (`tools/gen_tables.cc` writes
   `cjk/tables.hh`), not rewritten by hand: exact by construction; the generator fails on
   look-ahead, `{bof}` or 16-bit rows, so a new ICU cannot change behavior silently.
2. Thai, Lao, Khmer, Myanmar (Line_Break=Complex_Context) are out of scope: runs left whole.
   The sweep counts their differences apart (868 texts); the quest names Chinese, Japanese and
   Korean only.
3. Invalid UTF-8 reads as ISO-8859-1 one byte at a time (`unicode::next_utf8`, as
   term_generator does); ICU reads U+FFFD. Valid text is exact.
4. The model is ICU after its Chinese/Japanese engine loaded; ICU skips a process's first
   dictionary run as unhandled when it starts on U+30FC, U+FF70, U+FF9E or U+FF9F. The oracle
   loads the engine first ("日本語"). Reproducing a per-process load order is not behavior worth
   copying.
5. Other dictionary characters (Hangul, Common) are skipped statelessly like ICU's
   UnhandledEngine; ICU ships no Korean dictionary, so Hangul runs stay whole, matching ICU.
6. The dictionary reuses Kronuz/fst (pinned 7338f564) over dense character ids rather than a
   new trie: 2,087,781 bytes built in vs ICU's 2,007,142 (4.0% larger); alternatives measured
   in docs/cjk-segmenter.md. A rank-indexed trie (~1.4 MB est.) would belong in Kronuz/fst.
7. `data/cjdict.txt` is ICU 78.3's file unchanged; the FST is generated at build time
   (`tools/cjdict_gen.cc`), not committed.
8. Corpora are public domain (Lu Xun 吶喊 and 紅樓夢 from Gutenberg, Sōseki こころ from Aozora
   Bunko, Yi Kwang-su 무정 from Korean Wikisource), 110,000 characters each, with sources in
   `test/corpora/README.md`; `tools/fetch_corpora.py` rebuilds them.
9. Two mutants are equivalent for ICU 78.3's data and not run: katakana group limit 20 to 21
   (a 20-katakana run at 8,192 always loses to 20 unknowns at 255) and max word units 20 to
   19 (the longest word is 16 characters).
